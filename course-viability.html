Course Viability Overview

Visual Arts Department · Texas Woman’s University · Fall 2021 — Fall 2026 · 11 terms

The large majority of sections are healthy

Out of 499 active instructional sections analyzed across 11 terms, 457 met or exceeded the applicable enrollment minimum — a 91.6% overall viability rate. This report documents the 42 sections that fell short, where they fell, and how close they were to the threshold.

Shortfalls per Term

Number of sections below minimum enrollment per term. Spring 2023 had none.

How Far Below Threshold?

The majority of shortfalls were close — within one or two students of the minimum. Two-thirds of all cases were within 2 students, making them the most actionable through targeted advising or light scheduling adjustments.

Bracketed Pairs

When sections are bracketed, enrollment from both sections combines toward a shared minimum of 12. The department used bracketing in 9 of 11 terms, creating 19 UG/Grad pairs. Fourteen of those pairs — 74% — met or exceeded the combined threshold.

Areas for Continued Attention

Three patterns emerged consistently across the data. None represent crises, but all are worth monitoring in scheduling and advising.

  • Art Education track. Art Education courses (Elementary, Secondary, Principles, Approaches) appeared below the 12-student minimum 6 times across 5 terms. This reflects the broader BFA Art Education enrollment trend documented in the enrollment analysis and is something advising and recruitment efforts can address directly.
  • ART 3393 — Prof Practices Visual Arts. This required upper-division course fell below 12 in three separate terms (Sp 22, Fa 22, Fa 26). Because it serves multiple tracks, enrollment depends on how many students are at the right stage across programs simultaneously. It is worth flagging during pre-registration advising each term.
  • Fall semesters carry more risk than spring. Seven of the eleven terms had shortfalls only in fall. Fall 2021, 2022, and 2025 each had seven sections below minimum. Fall planning — particularly for upper-division electives with smaller pools — should account for this pattern.
